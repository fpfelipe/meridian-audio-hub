Unit 11

In global business, fluent vocabulary is not enough: professionals must master the delicate art of conversational rapport, mitigating direct statements with question tags and navigating the tricky syntax of multi-word phrasal verbs. Unit outcomes · CEFR B1Understand: Decipher subtle nuances of politeness, hedging, and indirect communication in international business meetings.Communicate: Check shared understanding and build conversational rapport using Question Tags (isn't it?, have they?) and appropriate intonation.Produce: Write a 120-word cross-cultural meeting protocol guide for a Brazilian delegation visiting international partners.Interpret: Interpret soft indirect disagreement ("That might be challenging") without mistaking it for agreement.Action: Chair an alignment session with an international partner while maintaining rapport and clarifying action items with phrasal verbs. MERIDIAN · Volume 03 · B1 · Unit 111 MERIDIANEveryday Life · Cross-Cultural Pragmatics · Reading RoomB1 · Unit 11 Reading RoomThe Anatomy of the Polite "No": Pragmatics Across Global BoardroomsIntercultural pragmatics study analyzing executive dialogues between Tokyo, Frankfurt, London, and São Paulo. In multinational executive negotiations, language proficiency is frequently measured not by grammatical complexity, but by pragmatic competence—the ability to align meaning, social distance, and cultural expectations. When professionals from Brazil, Germany, Japan, and the United Kingdom interact in English, misunderstandings rarely arise from obscure vocabulary or technical definitions. For instance, when a São Paulo commercial director presents an infrastructure proposal to British and German partners in London, friction often occurs because each business tradition encodes politeness, directness, and interpersonal deference through entirely divergent communicative conventions.
