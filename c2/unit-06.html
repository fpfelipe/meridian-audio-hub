In the matter of Sovereign Holdings versus Trans-Oceanic Energy. Sir Geoffrey, you represent the Claimant seeking a declaration of valid contract termination under Article 11. Please proceed. Sir Geoffrey Finch: May it please the Tribunal. The text of Article 11 is unequivocal. It establishes that in the event of any regulatory disruption degrading the target company’s prospective EBITDA by more than twenty percent, the Acquirer may rescind the agreement. When the ministry imposed a thirty-five percent tariff on lithium refining, our client’s financial modeling was eviscerated. Furthermore, Section 14 stipulates: 'Notwithstanding anything to the contrary herein contained or implied, the Purchaser’s right of termination shall remain absolute.' That overriding operator sweeps away all opposing arguments. Lord Justice Alistair Ross: A formidable submission, Sir Geoffrey. Ms. Sterling, how does the Respondent answer the plain language of Section 14? Ms. Sarah Sterling: With great respect to my learned friend, he is tearing Section 14 entirely out of its contractual enclosure! If the Tribunal reads the recitals and the operative preamble, it becomes manifest that Section 14 resides exclusively within the environmental indemnity schedule. 'Notwithstanding anything to the contrary herein' means herein within Schedule C—the contaminated soil remediation protocol! It was never intended to govern the general closing conditions of Article 11. To permit the Claimant to invoke an environmental override to escape an ordinary macroeconomic tariff shift would be a catastrophic perversion of Common Law precedent. Lord Justice Alistair Ross: The Tribunal has deliberated. Under the four corners doctrine, an operative operator cannot be deployed as an unconstrained wrecking ball against clear bilateral risk allocations. The tariff shift falls within the general industry carve-out of Article 11. The application for rescission is dismissed. ```
