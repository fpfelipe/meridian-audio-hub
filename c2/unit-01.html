The epistemological crux before this panel can be framed with Tractarian precision: when a state-of-the-art transformer predicts the next token with ninety-nine percent statistical coherence, does that output constitute a proposition capable of truth-value, or is it an elaborate semantic illusion? My position is unequivocal: absent intentionality, the system produces mere acoustic or typographic marks. It cannot but remain a closed syntactic loop. Dr. Miranda Chen: With respect, Alistair, that austere atomism reflects a century-old philosophical prejudice. While it stands to reason that initial layers compute token distributions, high-dimensional latent space representations tell a fundamentally different story. That empirical convergence warrants the inference that the network has constructed a functional model of the world. Dr. Alistair Finch: A topological map is not a mind, Miranda. A printed atlas maps the Alps, yet no one credits the ink with understanding altitude. There is no gainsaying that an AI lacks what Wittgenstein called a 'form of life.' It risks neither life nor limb; it possesses no biological vulnerability; it has no skin in the epistemic game. Where there are no existential stakes, can there truly be assertion? Dr. Miranda Chen: You are demanding biological embodiment as an arbitrary prerequisite for meaning. If an artificial intelligence can navigate complex counterfactual scenarios, arbitrate legal precedents, and formulate novel mathematical conjectures, one is hard-pressed to deny that it participates in our linguistic games. The boundary between syntactic calculation and genuine conceptual understanding is not a rigid wall; it is a gradient of cognitive sophistication. ```
