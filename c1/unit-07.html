Two thousand years ago, the Stoic philosopher Hierocles described human ethical obligations as a series of concentric circles: the innermost circle encompassing one's own mind, followed by family, neighbors, fellow citizens, and ultimately the entire human race. Dr. O'Connor, how can Stoic cosmopolitanism guide multilateral diplomacy in an age of resurging nationalism?
D Dr. O'Connor Interlocutor
🌐 International English
Hierocles argued that our moral task is deliberately to draw the outer circles inward, treating the stranger as a kinsperson and the global citizen as a compatriot. When catastrophic humanitarian crises strike, national boundaries must not become ethical moats.
S Secretary-General Interlocutor
🌐 International English
Yet populists argue that universal humanitarian obligations dilute civic loyalty to one's sovereign nation-state.
D Dr. O'Connor Interlocutor
🌐 International English
That is a false dichotomy. A mature cosmopolitan does not repudiate local affections; rather, she grounds them in universal ethical principles. We cannot defend domestic civil liberties while turning a blind eye to crimes against humanity abroad. Universal human dignity is indivisible.
