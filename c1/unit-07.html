To claim that sovereign borders permit neighboring states to turn away climate refugees with indifference is to commit moral bankruptcy. Ancient Stoicism established that reason is universal; modern human rights jurisprudence confirms that dignity is non-negotiable. Hierocles taught us that moral maturation means drawing the outer circles of humanity inward. Just as municipal laws yield to constitutional principles, so must sovereign prerogatives yield to universal humanitarian obligations.

DAME HELEN VANCE: With great respect, Tariq, that is noble philosophy, but hazardous statecraft. Democratic governance is inextricably anchored in a bounded political community. A parliament derives its legitimacy from a specific electorate that pays taxes, obeys laws, and consents to be governed. If you dissolve that boundary—if the state owes identical fiduciary obligations to eight billion people as it does to its own citizenry—you do not achieve universal fraternity; you trigger the catastrophic collapse of the welfare state and ignite violent xenophobic populism. Cosmopolitanism must operate through accountable multilateral treaties, not by disregarding the democratic sovereignty of national commonwealths. ```
