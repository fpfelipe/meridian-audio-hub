AUDIO_B2_U04_T01 **Unit:** B2-U04 (Urban Futures & Sustainability: Resilient Cities for 2050) ```text Host: Welcome to Urban Frontiers. Anouk, having retrofitted neighborhoods from Rotterdam to Wuhan, what is the core lesson of modern climate adaptation? Dr. Van Dijk: The core lesson is humility. For a century, civil engineers practiced brutal hydrological denial: paving over every square meter with impermeable tarmac, then building subterranean pipes to flush rainwater into rivers. Faced with atmospheric rivers, those pipes act as bottlenecks. Water has nowhere to go but into living rooms. Host: And Rotterdam chose an opposite trajectory. Dr. Van Dijk: Precisely. Having accepted that water will enter the city, we designed urban sponges. Take the Benthemplein Water Square: on sunny days, it is a vibrant basketball court and amphitheater; during torrential downpours, it retains 1.7 million liters of water. Having absorbed the deluge, it slowly filters moisture back into soil aquifers. Furthermore, by retaining moisture, the vegetation mitigates the urban heat island effect during blistering summers. Green sponge infrastructure is not a luxury; it is basic survival engineering. ```
